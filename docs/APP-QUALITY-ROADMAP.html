Product Quality Roadmap

A practical plan for English Automaticity, DeutschFlow, Study Tracker, Settings, PDF Studio, and Conversation Studio—viewed through the needs of a teacher, learner, and product manager.

Evidence boundary. Runtime, build, test, deployment, keyboard-semantic, and 320px/200% reflow findings are confirmed where stated. Human screen-reader and learner/teacher observation sessions are still required before claiming assisted-technology or learning-outcome compliance.

1. Product critique by role

Teacher perspective

Teach, observe, intervene

P0“Completed” must distinguish attempted work from checked mastery. A teacher needs the evidence, correction, and next intervention—not only a progress percentage.
P1Generated starter material must be visibly labelled as original draft content, editable before assignment, and attributable to a level, skill, and learning goal.
P1Long catalogues need compact filters, a searchable multi-select, and saved class views. A teacher should not scroll through 72 units to prepare one lesson.
Learner perspective

Know what to do next

P0Every practice card needs a stable layout at narrow widths: status, title, time, and “Open practice” must remain inside the card and touch-friendly.
P0Back navigation must return to the prior learning context with the selected unit, filter, scroll position, and unsaved safe draft preserved.
P1Show one clear reason for the next task: an error to repair, a due review, or a new goal. Avoid presenting seven equal choices without guidance.
Product manager perspective

Reliable delivery, clear ownership

P0Production readiness needs a repeatable gate: correct Vercel root directory, build success, public HTTP check, and route-level smoke test—not merely a green repository push.
P1Define one canonical product per job. Legacy dashboards should be marked “archive”, “prototype”, or “active” to prevent duplicated fixes and learner confusion.
P2Measure outcomes without weakening local-first privacy: anonymous feature health, task completion, recovery from errors, and opt-in learning evidence—not raw learner content by default.

3. CEFR model and data strategy

Decision 1 · learning safety

Three bounded capabilities

Classifier: estimates the CEFR difficulty of a text with probabilities and confidence. Error analyzer: reports only high-confidence, teachable errors. Generator: creates exercises only from retrieved rules/examples and must pass validators. A learner level is inferred later from repeated skill evidence, never from one text.

Decision 2 · model portfolio

Specialised models, one common benchmark

Start with TF-IDF plus linguistic features as the transparent floor. Benchmark ModernBERT and RoBERTa on English, GBERT on German, and XLM-RoBERTa plus mDeBERTa-v3 on the same multilingual protocol. LLaMA/LoRA is an optional later research comparison, not the default classifier, because it costs more to train and explain.

Decision 3 · release gate

No metric without a traceable run

Every dashboard row must identify dataset snapshot, split manifest, model revision, seed, hardware, elapsed time, predictions, and evaluation output. Dataset downloads require an approved licence/provenance record. Synthetic fixtures may test code but may never appear as product or research performance.

Research abstention policy completed · 30 August 2026

Confidence-gated abstention is now a measured threshold rule, not a roadmap sentence

A confidence-threshold policy declines a prediction when the model's own top probability falls below a validated floor, instead of reporting a false exact CEFR level. Applied to every measured run's real held-out test predictions (already written by training, not resimulated): none of the five per-language models reach 80% accuracy even limited to their most confident 30% of predictions, ranging 61% (English feature baseline) to 76% (German GBERT) at their best achievable threshold. Only the two pooled English+German multilingual models clear an 80%-accuracy/30%-coverage bar. This is the evidence that the per-language classifiers are not yet ready to support even a conservative confidence-gated feature in either learner app — a finding, not a launch decision. A pre-existing test that asserted only the synthetic fixture could be approved was updated to match this session's real corpus approvals instead of silently failing.
